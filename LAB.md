### Purchase phases and access gates

1. **Before/through 01:** DMM, passive/discrete parts, breadboard and safe low-voltage source. One ESP32 can wait until a stimulus is useful. Finish the simulation part of Lab 01 first.
2. **Before 02–03's physical waveforms:** arrange scope access. Buy one only after learning its triggering, probe, grounding and export requirements. A logic analyser cannot reveal analog ringing, slope, clipping or supply noise.
3. **Before 04–10:** acquire the photodiode, initial op amps and logic parts. Arrange current-limited supply access. Add soldering tools when moving from breadboard to adapters.
4. **Before 11–13:** add a verified logic analyser, second board, and external ADC. Buy breakouts with published schematics; check their pull-ups, regulators and actual logic voltage.
5. **Before 14–15:** buy the Pi and differential-link parts. Keep its acquisition workload small; a high-end Pi is unnecessary for a slow instrument.
6. **Before 16–18:** buy fabrication only after schematic/footprint review and measured front-end validation. Leave funds for connectors, enclosure and corrections.

## Reference hardware and component choices

**MCU.** Use a documented ESP32 development board. The reference for board-specific reading is [ESP32-DevKitC V4](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dev-kits/en/latest/esp32/esp32-devkitc/user_guide.html), preferably a clearly identified WROOM module variant. Another ESP32 family member is fine after checking its peripherals, pin restrictions, debug interface and toolchain support. Do not transfer pin numbers, ADC limitations, DAC availability, or USB features between variants by analogy. The two-board exercises are easiest with identical boards.

**Linux host.** A [Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-zero-2-w/) can serve as a modest headless logger for this slow instrument; a Pi 3/4/5 is also suitable. Allow for storage, a reliable supply, data/host adapters and any headers. Keep large numerical exploration on your existing computer if the Pi is memory-constrained. A Raspberry Pi Pico is a microcontroller, not a substitute for the Linux host in Module 14.

**Analog parts.** Start with 1% metal-film resistors, ceramic decoupling capacitors, suitable larger capacitors, an ordinary small-signal diode, resistor-limited LEDs, a small BJT, a small MOSFET, and a low-voltage rail-to-rail op amp in an accessible package. A [MCP6002](https://www.microchip.com/en-us/product/MCP6002) is a practical initial op-amp candidate; check the actual package pinout and electrical limits. The final TIA choice must follow measured bias, noise, bandwidth and stability requirements. Precision is not established by the words “rail-to-rail.”

**Detector and converter.** The [BPW34](https://www.vishay.com/docs/81521/bpw34.pdf) is a documented leaded photodiode candidate. The [ADS1115](https://www.ti.com/product/ADS1115) provides an accessible I²C external-ADC path for a slow instrument. Buy a breakout with an identifiable device and published schematic. Bring the ADC, its digital pull-ups and MCU interface to compatible 3.3 V levels. Keep the converter choice provisional until the sampling/filter requirements have been analysed.

**Digital parts.** Use logic families with documented operation at your rail voltage. A Schmitt-trigger gate is useful for the debounce lab. For SPI, a documented peripheral such as a small ADC can be added; loopback and a serial shift-register exercise are the lower-cost alternative. Check analyser input voltage limits and [actual sigrok support](https://sigrok.org/wiki/Getting_started_with_a_logic_analyzer) before buying. Marketing sample rate is not a guarantee of usable analog bandwidth or safe input range.

**Differential links.** Use transceivers whose supply, driver-input and receiver-output levels match the MCU. A controller peripheral does not directly drive a cable. A common inexpensive 5 V module is not automatically safe for 3.3 V inputs. Core CAN work is classical CAN on a compatible target, not an assumed CAN-FD capability.

See [PlatformIO's official Espressif platform documentation](https://docs.platformio.org/en/stable/platforms/espressif32.html).

### Initial environment inspection

At course creation, this computer had KiCad 10.0.8, PlatformIO Core 6.2.0, Python 3.13.11, NumPy, Matplotlib and pandas. An ngspice shared library was present, but no standalone ngspice executable was found. KiCad's simulator still needs a simple operating-point smoke check on your actual installation. SciPy, SymPy, Jupyter and the optional specialist tools were not found in the inspected environment. Nothing was installed or reconfigured while creating this handbook.

Start with the existing GUI simulator. For primitive circuits, use SPICE simulation symbols and an explicit ground. Check component-to-model pin mapping, model licence and simulation type. A converged simulation verifies the equations of the selected model, not the truth of its physical assumptions. Record model limitations, particularly noise, saturation recovery, switching behaviour and temperature coverage.

### Proprietary boundary

Your development, simulation and analysis route stays open source. Ordinary board operation may rely on vendor binaries: [Raspberry Pi documents its firmware sources](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/software-sources.html), and [Espressif publishes RF binary libraries](https://github.com/espressif/esp32-wifi-lib). Instrument firmware and some SPICE models can also have separate licences. Keep these exceptions explicit. No required assignment depends on a proprietary desktop IDE or simulator, and the readings do not require buying a textbook.

## Operating rules for these labs

The core experiments use low-voltage, low-energy circuits, normally 3.3 V or 5 V and only higher enclosed low-voltage rails when a reviewed design requires them. Use certified enclosed adapters; there are no exposed-mains, lithium-cell charging, high-power or laser experiments in the core.

- Wire with power off. Check rails, polarity, pinouts and resistance between supply and return before energising. Limit available current for first power-up and increase only after the measured load is understood.
- Measure voltage in parallel and current in series, using the correct meter jack/range. Return the lead to its voltage jack afterwards. Never use resistance/continuity mode on a powered circuit. Infer small currents from a known resistor drop when practical.
- Treat scope ground clips and instrument grounds as potentially connected to protective earth or to each other. Establish their topology from the manuals and continuity checks. Do not lift protective earth or attach a ground clip to an arbitrary powered node. Use correctly rated differential methods when a measurement genuinely requires them.
- Confirm the GPIO and analyser voltage limits. Do not feed a 5 V signal into a 3.3 V-only input. Use suitable level translation; resistor dividers are not a universal solution for bidirectional buses.
- Choose one documented power-input path per board unless its manual explicitly permits the combination. Avoid back-powering through GPIO, USB, or signal cables. Check common references when connecting externally powered devices.
- Limit LED and diode currents; check resistor and transistor dissipation. Discharge capacitors before rewiring. Account for inductive flyback where relevant.
- Use eye protection and local fume extraction/ventilation for soldering, keep the iron in its stand, and wash hands after handling solder/flux. Clean high-impedance circuitry appropriately; residue and moisture can become part of the signal path.

## Measurement record

For each physical capture, save circuit revision, component identities/values, supply setting and measured rail, ambient conditions where relevant, probe attenuation and connection, scope/analyser bandwidth and sample settings, units, sample-clock source, and raw data. Keep calibration coefficients and their uncertainties separate from raw readings.

Prefer a short reproducible report over a large collection of screenshots. A spreadsheet is optional; CSV plus a small analysis notebook/script is sufficient. Always state whether a result is derived, simulated, measured, bounded by instrument capability, or still unverified.
